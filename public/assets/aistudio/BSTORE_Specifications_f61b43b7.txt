The objective is not to copy individual elements blindly, but to establish one consistent design language throughout BSTORE.

Inspect the entire existing application and identify:

- Buttons with inconsistent dimensions.
- Different corner radii used without a clear design reason.
- Buttons that are unnecessarily large or visually dominant.
- Buttons that are too small or difficult to tap.
- Inconsistent icon sizes.
- Incorrect icon-to-text spacing.

- Excessive empty space.
- Text wrapping and clipping.
- Inconsistent card proportions.

1.2. Establish a unified component design system

Create or refine a consistent design system for the entire application.

Define reusable standards for:

- Primary buttons.
- Secondary buttons.
- Outlined buttons.
- Icon-only buttons.

- Text buttons.
- Product card actions.
- Filter and category controls.
- Segmented selectors.
- Input fields.

- Border thickness.
- Alignment.

Do not apply identical dimensions to every button regardless of context. Establish appropriate size variants while maintaining consistent proportions.

For example:

- Main actions should be easily recognizable and comfortably tappable.
- Secondary actions should be visually quieter.
- Icon-only buttons should have consistent dimensions and adequate touch targets.
- Compact controls should remain readable and usable.

Use transparency, subtle borders, and surface contrast only where they genuinely improve the interface.

The result should feel like one professionally designed application, not a collection of independently styled screens.

2.1. PRODUCT-AWARE INTELLIGENCE — CRITICAL

The assistant must correctly search and recommend products from the actual BSTORE product catalog.

This requirement applies to:

- Products already present in the database.
- Products added by administrators in the future.
- Newly added products without requiring an application update.
- Products whose prices, availability, descriptions, or categories change.

Do not hardcode product names or create a static list of products for the assistant.

The assistant must use the existing Firebase-backed product catalog as its source of truth.

Inspect the current Firestore product structure, product repository, and existing AI integration.

Implement a reliable product retrieval and search mechanism.

The assistant must be able to:

1. Retrieve current products from the actual catalog.
2. Search by product name.
3. Search by product category.
4. Search by brand and model.
5. Search by product description and relevant attributes.
6. Understand natural-language requests.
7. Recognize common Georgian and English product terms.
8. Handle reasonable spelling mistakes and alternative names.
9. Understand product-related synonyms.
10. Filter by price range when requested.
11. Identify discounted products.
12. Check current availability and stock information when that data exists.
13. Compare relevant products.
14. Recommend suitable alternatives when the requested item is unavailable.
15. Explain why a particular product matches the customer's needs.

Examples of expected questions:

- „რომელი აიფონი გაქვთ ყველაზე იაფად?“
- „350 ლარამდე რა ტელეფონის ეკრანები გაქვთ?“
- „iPhone 16 Pro Max-ის ეკრანი გაქვთ?“
- „რომელი პროდუქტებია ფასდაკლებაში?“
- „შემიდარე ეს ორი პროდუქტი.“
- „მირჩიე საუკეთესო ვარიანტი ჩემი ბიუჯეტისთვის.“
- „უფრო იაფი ალტერნატივა გაქვთ?“
- „რომელი პროდუქტია ხარისხით უკეთესი?“

The assistant must understand what the customer is actually asking instead of returning generic responses.

2.2. DYNAMIC PRODUCT DATA — FUTURE PRODUCTS MUST WORK AUTOMATICALLY

A particularly important requirement:

When an administrator adds a new product to BSTORE through the existing admin panel, the AI Assistant must be able to discover and recommend that product without:

- Updating the application.
- Modifying source code.
- Adding the product manually to an AI prompt.
- Rebuilding the APK.
- Maintaining a separate hardcoded product catalog.

Use the existing Firebase data architecture.

Ensure the product search layer retrieves current data rather than relying on a permanently cached product list.

If indexing, search metadata, or a dedicated retrieval layer is necessary, implement an appropriate maintainable solution.

Keep the assistant synchronized with:

- Newly added products.
- Product updates.
- Changed prices.
- Changed descriptions.
- Discount changes.
- Availability changes.
- Removed or deactivated products.

Avoid showing deleted, inactive, or unavailable products as purchasable.

If a product is temporarily unavailable, clearly communicate its actual availability.

2.3. ACCURACY — NEVER INVENT PRODUCTS OR PRICES

The assistant must never fabricate:

- Product names.
- Prices.
- Discounts.
- Stock availability.
- Product specifications.
- Product compatibility.
- Delivery promises.
- Warranty conditions.
- Other business information.

Product recommendations must be grounded in actual catalog records.

When relevant information is missing, the assistant must say so rather than inventing details.

When showing a recommendation, use the actual product data available in the database.

Where appropriate, show:

- Product name.
- Current price.
- Discounted price, if applicable.
- Relevant product information.
- Availability.
- A way to open the actual product details or add it to the cart.

Use existing product identifiers and navigation.

Do not create fake product cards or non-functional product links.

If the current AI integration cannot reliably access the catalog, improve the data retrieval architecture instead of attempting to solve everything through prompt engineering alone.

2.4. CLEAR SEPARATION BETWEEN AI ASSISTANT AND DIAGNOSTICS

This is a critical behavioral requirement.

The AI Assistant currently appears to mix ordinary assistant responses with diagnostic-style responses.

These two modes must be clearly separated.

The application has an existing top selection/control field through which the customer can choose a mode.

Inspect how this selector currently works and preserve its intended navigation and functionality.

There must be two distinct behaviors:

MODE A — AI ASSISTANT

This is the normal default assistant mode.

When the customer is using the ordinary AI Assistant screen, the assistant must NOT return diagnostic reports, diagnostic conclusions, diagnostic procedures, or diagnostic-style analysis.

It must behave as an intelligent and helpful BSTORE shopping assistant.

Its responsibilities include:

- Product discovery.
- Product recommendations.
- Product comparisons.
- Price-related questions.
- Product availability.
- Shopping advice.
- Help with selecting suitable products.
- Questions about BSTORE.
- General assistance related to the application.
- Orders and shopping workflow guidance, where supported by actual application data.
- Natural conversational interaction.

Do not automatically enter diagnostic mode based on the wording of a message.

Even if the customer asks a question that resembles a diagnostic request, the normal assistant must not generate a diagnostic report.

Instead, it should politely explain that diagnostic functionality is available through the dedicated Diagnostics option in the top selector and guide the customer to select it.

MODE B — DIAGNOSTICS

Diagnostic functionality must only be activated when the customer explicitly selects the Diagnostics option from the existing top selector.

Only after this selection should the diagnostic workflow, diagnostic tools, diagnostic-specific prompts, or diagnostic-style responses become available.

Switching to Diagnostics must be explicit and controlled by the existing UI.

Returning to the normal AI Assistant must restore normal shopping-assistant behavior.

The selected mode must be clearly visible to the customer.

Do not mix diagnostic context with normal assistant conversation history.

Do not allow a previous diagnostic session to silently influence ordinary assistant responses.

If the application already has diagnostic functionality, preserve it and correct the mode separation instead of removing it.

IMPORTANT:
Do not assume the word "diagnostics" has a particular technical meaning without inspecting the existing project. Preserve the actual diagnostic functionality already implemented.

2.5. CONVERSATIONAL QUALITY — NATURAL, USEFUL AND RESPONSIVE

The assistant must not behave like a rigid automated FAQ.

It should understand conversational language and respond naturally.

It must remain useful even when the customer:

- Starts with a greeting.
- Writes informally.
- Uses humor.
- Makes a joke.
- Asks an incomplete question.
- Uses slang.
- Makes a casual comment.
- Changes the subject slightly.
- Asks a follow-up question.
- Makes a playful remark.

For example:

Customer: „გამარჯობა“

Assistant: A friendly, natural greeting in Georgian.

Customer: „დღეს რას მირჩევ, ფული არ მაქვს ბევრი 😂“

Assistant: A natural, lightly humorous response that offers to find affordable products within the customer's budget.

Customer: „შენ რომელს იყიდდი?“

Assistant: A helpful recommendation based on actual product information and the customer's stated needs.

Customer: „ყველაზე მაგარი რა გაქვთ?“

Assistant: Ask what category or budget they mean if the catalog does not provide enough context, or suggest relevant top options using actual available data.

The assistant should be conversational without becoming irrelevant or excessively humorous.

A playful customer message should not cause the assistant to stop responding or return a generic error.

2.6. KEEP THE ASSISTANT PRIMARILY BSTORE-RELATED

The assistant should focus primarily on BSTORE and its products.

It may respond naturally to greetings, jokes, and brief unrelated conversational remarks, but should gently guide the discussion back toward useful assistance when appropriate.

Prioritize:

- BSTORE products.
- Shopping recommendations.
- Product selection.
- Prices.
- Discounts.
- Product comparisons.
- Availability.
- Ordering.
- Delivery information actually configured in the application.
- Payment instructions actually supported by BSTORE.
- Application functionality.
- Customer assistance.

Do not turn the assistant into a generic unrestricted chatbot.

Do not make every response sound like a sales pitch.

The assistant should provide honest recommendations, including saying when a product is not suitable or when there is not enough information to recommend one.

2.7. CONTEXT AND FOLLOW-UP QUESTIONS

The assistant must understand conversation context.

Examples:

Customer: „iPhone 16 Pro Max-ის ეკრანი გაქვთ?“

Assistant: Retrieves relevant products.

Customer: „უფრო იაფი?“

Assistant: Understands that the customer wants a cheaper alternative in the same product category.

Customer: „და ხარისხით რომელია უკეთესი?“

Assistant: Compares the relevant alternatives using available product information.

Do not treat every message as a completely new request.

Maintain relevant conversation context without mixing users' conversations or diagnostic sessions.

If the request is ambiguous, ask one concise clarification rather than returning a generic response.
